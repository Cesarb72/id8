# Part I — The Product

## 1. What ID.8 is

ID.8 is a personal concierge for real-world experiences. It helps a person move from a loose desire—*we should do something*—to a plan that feels deliberate, possible, and worth following.

Most discovery products help people find options. Most planning tools help them record decisions already made. ID.8 closes the difficult space between them: interpreting what kind of experience fits, finding real opportunities, coordinating those opportunities into a sequence, proving that sequence can work, and preserving it when reality changes.

ID.8 is not a list of venues, a local-search skin, a random activity generator, or a route optimizer with charming copy. Its value is the composed experience: the right places, in the right roles, in a sequence that builds, peaks, and resolves without asking the person to become their own concierge.

> **ID.8 builds your plan better than you can—without taking the plan away from you.**

### ID.8 and Arc

Arc is the engine system. ID.8 is its first consumer application and proving ground.

- People experience **ID.8**.
- ID.8 expresses truth produced through **Arc**.
- Arc does not dictate the interface or Product personality.
- ID.8 does not rewrite engine truth because the interface wants an easier answer.

The application gives the system a human purpose; the system gives the application reliable coordination.

## 2. The human problem

People rarely begin with a perfect plan. They begin with a feeling, social moment, constraint, or fragment:

- *I want this to feel special.*
- *We want to get out, but nobody wants to decide.*
- *I have one thing I definitely want to do—build around it.*
- *Give us something we would not have found ourselves.*
- *Make it work without turning this into homework.*

Today, a person translates that intent across search, social posts, maps, reviews, calendars, ticketing, group messages, saved lists, and local knowledge. Every tool exposes another partial truth. The person still has to decide what fits, whether the pieces belong together, what order makes sense, how much movement is tolerable, what happens if something closes, and whether the whole plan will feel good.

| Burden | What the person is forced to do |
|---|---|
| **Expression** | Turn a feeling into searchable categories and filters. |
| **Discovery** | Separate meaningful possibilities from abundant but generic options. |
| **Composition** | Decide which pieces form one experience rather than a pile of stops. |
| **Coordination** | Resolve time, distance, sequence, dependencies, and real-world friction. |
| **Confidence** | Trust that the reviewed plan is the plan they will actually get. |

The burden is also social and relational. Someone has to initiate, search, compare, ask for agreement, remember the idea, reconcile schedules, account for money and movement, confirm the choice, and keep the plan from dissolving between intention and action. The person carrying that work may be trying to create connection while simultaneously managing the logistics of connection.

ID.8 must reduce unresolved decisions and handoffs—not merely recommend something appealing. It should help people move from desire to credible possibility, from possibility to a coherent plan, from plan to understandable commitment, and from commitment to an experience that still holds in the world.

### Catalyst and dependency patterns

Early research identifies two recurring planning conditions:

- A catalyst already exists—an occasion, event, destination, craving, activity, or desired change of scenery—and the person needs the experience composed around it.
- The absence of a catalyst is itself the burden, and the person needs a credible idea that can begin the night.

Research also shows that plans may remain provisional while gating dependencies resolve. Childcare, schedule, health, energy, budget, availability, weather, transportation, and next-day obligations can change what is possible before venue choice is useful. ID.8 must distinguish hard gates from ordinary preferences and coordinate the sequence in which reality becomes knowable.

The value is not automation for its own sake. It is returning time, attention, confidence, and shared presence to the people having the experience.

## 3. The Product promise

### Light input

The person should not need to describe their life, complete a profile, or manipulate a control panel before receiving value. ID.8 asks only for information needed to make the current experience better.

### Complete experience

The output is a plan, not a result. Each stop has a role. The sequence has pacing. Movement and timing make sense. A required anchor survives. The ending belongs to the same experience as the beginning.

### Structured spontaneity

ID.8 creates possibility beyond the obvious while preserving coherence. Surprise is bounded cognitive offload, not chaos.

### Trustworthy continuity

The plan the person chooses must survive preview, review, lock, save, share, and live use. When conditions change, ID.8 should preserve the committed intent through proportionate, authorized adaptation. When it cannot prove a usable route, it must say so honestly and help the person continue.

## 4. Product principles

1. **Human value is the reason for the system.** Architecture matters because it protects the experience.
2. **Discovery is the front door.** Vague intent should become legible without pressure, noise, or premature commitment.
3. **A plan is a sequence, not a bag of places.** Quality is relational and belongs to the whole composition.
4. **Surprise must remain coherent.** Non-obvious does not mean arbitrary.
5. **Steering should feel consequential.** The person should understand what is preserved and recomputed.
6. **Confidence comes from continuity, not confident copy.** Reviewed, committed, saved, shared, and live routes require one authorized lineage.
7. **Honest failure is a safety requirement, not the finish line.** The goal remains approved, usable plans.
8. **Agency means good choices, not exposed machinery.** People steer outcomes without debugging the system.
9. **Local specificity matters.** Plans unfold through real pockets, blocks, transitions, and moments.
10. **Trust precedes growth and monetization.** Partnerships and placement may never become shadow recommendation authority.

# Part II — People, Needs, and Scope

## 5. Who ID.8 serves

The current Product is organized around social context and experience intent, not demographic caricatures. Its three scenario families are **Romantic**, **Friends**, and **Family**. Each represents a different human promise and constraint posture; none is a fixed personality model.

- **Romantic** may require intimacy, anticipation, occasion, conversation, novelty, and graceful resolution without inferring relationship stage, gender roles, or spending norms.
- **Friends** may require shared energy, play, conversation, discovery, collaboration, or flexible group pacing without reducing friendship to nightlife.
- **Family** may require participation, accessibility, caregiving, safety, attention span, early timing, and lower transfer tolerance without creating a persona-owned constraint engine.

Human context may change what a good plan requires. It does not authorize stereotypes. Claims about unresearched people remain hypotheses with an affected population, evidence gap, inclusion risk, and validation plan.

## 6. Experience expression

ID.8 currently proves breadth through nine scenario families formed by three social contexts and three experience postures: **Cozy**, **Lively**, and **Cultured**.

These labels are starting language, not a complete emotional ontology. They must create distinct experience promises rather than decorative themes or hard-coded venue categories.

Every supported scenario requires equal depth: a clear promise, distinctive expression, complete route shape, real and role-right stops, feasible movement and timing, truthful preview-to-commit continuity, and recovery when the original world cannot hold.

## 7. Durable user needs

Early research establishes durable signals that modern ID.8 must continue to validate:

- reduce decision labor without removing agency;
- bridge discovery into coordinated action;
- preserve possibilities without forcing commitment;
- make consequential actions unmistakable;
- keep one coherent mental model across surfaces;
- support bounded delegation when deciding is the burden;
- help plans survive attention and time;
- orient people without making them invent the right prompt;
- reveal the plan and its state at the moment of commitment;
- support the social handoff around a plan;
- begin with the reason for going out when one already exists;
- interpret the people and their vision before generating options;
- resolve viability as part of the recommendation;
- help participants reach agreement;
- distinguish gating dependencies from ordinary preferences;
- preserve accumulated interests and lived history without trapping people in static profiles;
- optimize for memorable coherence, not mere availability;
- reduce coordination anxiety, not only search time.

Historical usability work is valuable evidence of comprehension and workflow problems. It does not prove present usability, present demand, prevalence, or lived impact. Requests for calendars, reminders, booking, favorites, or sharing must be preserved both as literal requests and as evidence of underlying coordination needs; they do not automatically become committed features.

## 8. Scope and proving ground

San Jose is the proving ground. It is not a disposable demo dataset and not yet evidence of national readiness.

- **Demo Ready:** a complete flow that does not lie and holds through shared preview and review.
- **MVP / Closed Beta:** a real San Jose user can use every supported mode, receive a usable plan, save and resume it, and understand honest failure without the founder present.
- **Product Launch:** San Jose is proven through beta, infrastructure supports public use, the business model protects trust, and the architecture can expand without being rebuilt for each city.

The active tier sets the goal post. Capability must be proven in San Jose before opening city two.

# Part III — The Experience System

## 9. The three entry modes

The modes are sibling Product contracts that allocate initiative differently. They are not parallel products and may not create separate downstream truth spines.

| Mode | Human promise | Control posture | Required start | Pre-generation ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Surprise Me** | “Lead for me.” | System-led | One tap | No shaping inputs; location only when required to establish where the experience can occur |
| **Curate Experience** | “Help me choose a direction.” | Guided | Choose a meaningful starter | Up to two shaping inputs after the starter; missing location counts |
| **Build My Plan** | “Build around this.” | User-led | Choose an anchor | Up to three shaping inputs after anchor selection; missing area/location counts |

### Surprise Me — bounded delegation

> **Give me one strong night fast, then let me inspect it, try a genuinely different one, or continue.**

Surprise is system-led first and user-controlled after the proposal. Entry is one tap. The first honest output is one generated, authorized route—not a questionnaire, direction-picking exercise, simultaneous candidate board, or new source of truth.

Regenerate requests a materially different authorized route under the same governing input. Continue enters the shared preview. Any route-changing adjustment must return through owner-engine authorization.

### Curate Experience — guided direction

> **Start from a strong direction, shape it lightly, and let ID.8 turn that promise into a real night.**

Curate begins with a small set of emotionally legible starter contracts. A starter is not decorative copy, a vibe label, or a half-preview; it must carry an Interpretation-authored scenario signal that changes the promise being pursued.

Curate's meaningful choice occurs at the promise level. After selecting and lightly shaping a direction, the person receives one authorized route that fulfills it. Curate does not require comparison among several fully built nights after generation.

### Build My Plan — hard-anchor authorship

> **Start with the one thing you know you want, shape the request lightly, and let ID.8 build a coherent night that preserves it.**

Build is user-led first and system-executed second. It is the highest-control consumer posture, but not a blank itinerary builder, operator console, logistics dashboard, or stop-by-stop workshop.

The starting request may be an **exact place or event**, or a **kind of place or activity**. An exact place must resolve to the correct canonical identity; Field source identity remains provenance while Interpretation owns canonical identity. A kind-of-thing request must first resolve into an authorized anchor candidate and may not masquerade as a specific user-selected venue.

Once established, the anchor is hard through the Required Stop Contract. It cannot be dropped, weakened, silently replaced, or lost through generation, feasibility, authorization, Review, or Lock. If ID.8 cannot build a route that preserves it, Build refuses honestly or offers a governed recovery action.

Build produces one authorized route around the anchor. Alternatives and recovery routes must be regenerated and reauthorized while preserving the anchor. Anchor Search is a critical cross-engine seam, not a standalone engine or merely a search box; it requires its own bounded implementation specification before revision.

Move 3 closed the bounded Build readiness gap: soft-feasible recovery and contextual role-casting broaden usable anchor-led routes without weakening the anchor or overriding a role the person explicitly authored.

## 10. Canonical journey

1. **Mode entry and intent.** The person chooses a mode and supplies only what its contract requires. Interpretation authors the canonical intent contract.
2. **Promise or anchor establishment.** Surprise delegates the first route; Curate selects a starter/direction promise; Build establishes a hard anchor.
3. **Authorized route generation.** Arc produces and proves one route under the governing contract.
4. **Shared Plan Preview.** The person understands what was built, why it fits, and how it moves.
5. **Review and refinement.** The person inspects the actual lockable route. Authorized changes trigger fresh owner evaluation.
6. **Lock.** The reviewed route becomes committed only when required authority and Great Stop proof hold.
7. **Live.** The locked route is presented through runtime continuity. Route-changing adaptation requires owner-engine reauthorization; LCE coordinates but never authors.
8. **Save, share, and return.** Durable lifecycle behavior preserves stable identity, version, provenance, and lineage.

**Current build boundary:** Move 3 is closed. Browser-local continuity exists, but it is not durable Save. Account-backed Save → Return → Delete and immutable shared snapshots are ratified Move 4 contracts whose implementation has not begun. Governed post-lock route adaptation remains outside Move 4 under R-02. MVP-green is not claimed.

## 11. Preview, Review, Lock, and authority

| Moment | Human question | Product boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Entry choice** | “How do I want help?” | Select mode, starter/direction, or anchor according to the mode contract. |
| **Preview** | “What exactly did ID.8 build?” | Understand one authorized route and its rationale. |
| **Review** | “Is this the route I will lock?” | Inspect, refine, swap, and revalidate the lockable route. |
| **Lock** | “Commit this exact plan.” | Commit only the route reviewed and approved through the authority stack. |

One canonical surface owns mutation at a time. Application may present states and actions; it may not reconstruct routes from labels, partial objects, query parameters, or stale local state when a canonical artifact exists.

## 12. Steering and recovery

Steering is a request to recompute under changed intent or preference, not permission for Application to edit engine truth. The person should see what the product is preserving, what is changing, and when reassessment is required.

For Build soft-feasible recovery, when existing Bearings evidence shows that the hard anchor survives and only soft movement or structural constraints fail, Product may surface two governed actions:

- **Take the bigger night:** re-evaluate the exact surfaced route under a broader movement posture.
- **Try a tighter route:** perform a governed rebuild around the same hard anchor.

Neither action bypasses Bearings, weakens Great Stop, promotes a failed route, or drops the anchor. The choice binds to the exact route and evidence shown and expires if bound truth changes. Move 3 closed the governing recovery posture and contextual role-casting behavior; later copy refinement may not alter those contracts.

## 13. Home, Plans Hub, and continuity

Home routes. It helps a person continue an active experience, start a new one, or revisit prior work. It does not become a form, general discovery feed, or recommendation engine.

Plans Hub is the Application memory surface for active, locked, saved, and eventually versioned plan artifacts. It may present resume, revisit, duplicate, compare, restore, or branch only when the underlying lifecycle supports them. It does not infer taste, analyze saved plans, or become an engine.

The intended Home priority is: active Live plan; locked plan still in progress; recent saved plan; start a new plan; secondary discovery.

Provenance explains where a plan came from—Surprise, Curate, Build, starter, anchor, or context. It never becomes alternate route authority. Chosen, completed, skipped, unknown, and optional sentiment remain distinct outcome evidence.

## 14. Environment and routing doctrine

Routing separates **environment**, **Product state**, and **authority**.

- **Development Sandbox** is the proving ground for active Product and UX work.
- **Concierge** is the deliberately promoted, demo-ready surface—not an automatically synchronized copy.
- **Archive** preserves history and must never become an implicit entry, fallback, redirect, or Back destination.

Meaningful Product states do not each require their own permanent URL. Likewise, a URL does not become authoritative merely because it contains `mode`, `starterId`, `anchorId`, or `planId`. These may support navigation, restoration, and explicit origin; they do not own intent, identity, composition, feasibility, approval, commitment, or lineage.

There is no universal post-generation **Choose** stage. Surprise presents one system-selected route. Curate chooses a starter/direction before generation. Build establishes an anchor before generation. All three then converge on the same Preview → Review → Lock → Live lifecycle.

Exact paths, browser history, refresh recovery, deep links, share routes, and promotion mechanics require a later code-truth routing specification.

## 15. Voice and interaction character

ID.8 should feel like a calm, competent concierge with a touch of charm: prepared, perceptive, concise, and never theatrical about its own intelligence.

- Lead with the plan, not the machinery.
- Explain fit in human terms grounded in evidence.
- Use confidence proportionate to proof.
- Keep debug language, scores, reason codes, and engine names out of consumer flow.
- Prefer invitations and choices over warnings and dead ends.
- Give each surface one primary action and one legible role.
- Maps explain movement; story structure explains pacing; stop cards explain contribution.

# Part IV — Product Truth and the Arc System

## 16. Engine spine

| Engine | One-line job | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| **Field** | Detects current reality. | Source facts, provenance, retrieval, freshness, availability signals. |
| **Interpretation** | Turns signals into meaning. | Intent, Taste, scenario meaning, canonical identity resolution, promise semantics. |
| **Bearings** | Applies real-world constraints. | Feasibility, admissibility, timing, movement, hard/soft constraint verdicts. |
| **Waypoint** | Coordinates the sequence. | Selection, roles in sequence, pacing, route assembly, Great Stop aggregation. |
| **LCE** | Keeps the sequence aligned as reality changes. | Post-lock continuity and governed runtime coordination. |
| **Application** | Turns the stack into usable products. | Input capture, presentation, lifecycle orchestration, and human interaction—never engine truth. |

Each engine hands an owned result to the next seam. It does not reinterpret or enforce another engine's job. Application renders and orchestrates; it never repairs route truth.

## 17. Portable Arc pattern

Every Arc application begins with a human promise and carries it through a governed execution chain:

> **Ask → Promise → Shape → Reality → Coordination → Continuity**

1. Application captures the ask; Interpretation authors the canonical input contract.
2. Interpretation expresses the domain-specific promise.
3. The promise takes an ordered temporal shape.
4. Field senses relevant reality; Interpretation gives it meaning; Bearings determines where it can honestly occur.
5. Waypoint coordinates admitted possibilities into a coherent sequence.
6. After commitment, owner engines reassess material change and LCE coordinates continuity.

This pattern is Product/System doctrine, not authorization for new artifact names or schemas. Current PDD, architecture, and code truth govern implementation.

## 18. Bookend contracts and artifact truth

**ConciergeIntent** is the promise going in. It carries normalized intent, people, time, location, mode semantics, constraints, and required anchors without repeatedly reading raw UI state.

**Great Stop** is proof coming out. It aggregates owner-authored verdicts across realness, role-rightness, intent-rightness, feasibility, and copy readiness without re-authoring them.

The canonical ladder moves from raw input to intent, candidate contracts, the selected authorized route, Review, Lock, and lifecycle state. A new artifact earns canonical status only when it has one owner, one purpose, stable identity, deterministic construction, explicit consumers, and no competing source of truth.

Route-changing mutation requires fresh authorization. Identity, provenance, approval evidence, and ordered route shape must survive lifecycle transitions. Missing, mismatched, or stale evidence may not masquerade as approved truth.

## 19. Identity and provenance

- Field owns source identity and provenance.
- Interpretation owns canonical identity resolution.
- Bearings preserves identity while applying feasibility.
- Waypoint coordinates canonical entities without minting substitutes.
- Application references canonical identity without inventing it.

Provider-derived IDs may identify source records; they may not silently become canonical route identity. Same-place reconciliation must occur at the Interpretation-owned seam before downstream use.

## 20. Provider and data governance

Provider data enters through one governed Field adapter and remains non-authoritative until normalized and admitted. Static and live sources may enrich the same entity but do not create separate Product truths.

The provider valve remains closed until the active proof phase explicitly opens it. Fallback must be visible in provenance and may not impersonate current provider truth.

# Part V — Trust, Quality, and Learning

## 21. Product acceptance standard

A route is acceptable only when it is:

- **Real** — the stop exists with sufficient current-world grounding.
- **Role-Right** — each stop fulfills its authored role.
- **Intent-Right** — the route fulfills the human promise.
- **Feasible** — timing, movement, and hard constraints hold.
- **Copy-Ready** — Product can explain the route truthfully.

Experience acceptance also requires that all supported modes work, the route is meaningfully distinct, review equals lock, lock equals Live, the hard anchor survives, adjustments are consequential, failures are honest and recoverable, and a person can complete the flow without the founder present.

## 22. Failure and recovery doctrine

The system must stop honestly rather than:

- showing one route and locking another;
- treating invisible logic as a usable Product experience;
- allowing storage identities or surface copies to diverge;
- repairing engine truth inside Application;
- patching a local symptom before tracing the system seam;
- using broad central-surface fixes that hide ownership errors;
- presenting fixture or fallback data as live truth.

Root-cause work begins at the system level: identify engine ownership, trace the wire into the failure, map connected artifacts and surfaces, define the whole-system problem, and only then zoom into the local implementation.

Honest refusal is a safety state and diagnostic signal. Product success remains a valid, approved, usable plan.

## 23. Research and learning doctrine

The learning loop is:

> **Human context → evidence → Product interpretation → experience contract → engine behavior → shipped experience → observed outcome → revised understanding**

Evidence types are not interchangeable:

| Evidence | What it can support |
|---|---|
| **Direct user language** | Meaning, vocabulary, expectation, tension. |
| **Observed behavior** | Comprehension, effort, discoverability, breakdowns. |
| **Explicit outcome** | Chosen, completed, skipped, and optional sentiment. |
| **System proof** | Determinism, continuity, constraint enforcement, recovery. |
| **Founder judgment** | Product direction and values, clearly labeled. |
| **Market hypothesis** | Future opportunity requiring validation. |

Feedback is interpreted, not obeyed literally. Research must separate participant evidence from researcher synthesis, historical interface ideas from durable needs, and desired outcome from observed impact. Unknown is a valid result.

### Current research priorities

- Can first-time users understand the three modes without explanation?
- Do generated plans feel meaningfully distinct and worthwhile?
- Does Curate make promise-level choice clear?
- Does Build make hard-anchor preservation and recovery trustworthy?
- Do Preview and Review create commitment-level confidence?
- Are movement, timing, and the energy arc believable?
- Do recovery choices preserve agency and optimism?
- Does the reviewed plan hold in real life?
- What made the lived experience memorable, awkward, easy, or abandoned?
- Which contexts are missing from Romantic, Friends, and Family?

A new vertical supplies an Interpretation lens, a Field adapter, and an Application expression. # Part VII — Working Agreements

## 28. Product-to-build contract

Every handoff states:

- the human job and visible experience;
- the owner engine and canonical artifact seam;
- what may change and what must remain invariant;
- implementation and Product acceptance evidence;
- explicit exclusions and stop conditions.

Work follows a staged loop: one system-level exploratory audit, one scoped implementation, one bounded proof pass, and a decision. No unaimed cave dives.

Use local proof for application logic and iterative changes. Use hosted/Vercel proof only when provider, deployment, environment, or final Preview behavior requires it.

## 29. Decision framework

1. **Product integrity:** Is the root cause portable and owner-correct? Does the solution keep the architecture clean?
2. **Can this human use it?** Is the real-user flow sensible, engaging, and understandable?
3. **Pacing:** Is this MVP-critical, or should real feedback trigger it later?

Load-bearing or protected-seam work must be flagged. Cross-engine changes require explicit authorization. If investigation reveals a larger boundary than approved, stop and escalate.

## 30. Diagnostic protocol

1. Confirm branch, baseline, worktree, provider state, and accepted proof.
2. Read governing Product, decision, architecture, and build-state records.
3. Trace owner boundaries and the full wire seam into the failure.
4. Map connected systems, artifacts, and surfaces.
5. State the system-level problem before proposing a fix.
6. Separate architecture, Product, data, state, copy, and proof failures.
7. Implement only after authorization, then prove locally and update the record.

An audit may characterize and recommend; it may not author behavior or records without approval.

## Closing doctrine

ID.8 succeeds when a person can bring light intent, receive a plan that feels unexpectedly right, understand why it works, trust that it will hold, and experience the same promise through commitment and change.

Arc succeeds when that outcome is produced by a system whose responsibilities are clear, artifacts are canonical, evidence is honest, behavior is deterministic where required, and domain meaning can change without collapsing the invariant machine.

The Product and system meet at one standard:

> **Human meaning in. Coordinated reality out. One trustworthy line between them.**
